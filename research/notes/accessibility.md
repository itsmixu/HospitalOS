# Accessibility and Assistive Use Cases for AI/AR Smart Glasses (2023–2026)

## Blind / low vision: products, real tasks, limitations

### Takeaway
Camera-plus-audio glasses (no display needed) are the strongest-evidence accessibility category: Ray-Ban/Oakley Meta with Be My Eyes and "detailed responses", Envision Ally Solos, Aira, OOrion. Blind users mostly use them for reading (mail, menus, packaging, signs), identifying products, scene description and hands-free human help. The main limitations are AI that summarises when it should read verbatim, confident hallucinations, the need to learn how to phrase prompts, and the fact that the glasses add to a cane or guide dog rather than replace them.

### Cited Findings
**Meta + Be My Eyes**
- Be My Eyes launched on Ray-Ban Meta on 13 Nov 2024 (US, Canada, UK, Ireland, Australia). The trigger is "Hey Meta, Be My Eyes", which connects to a sighted volunteer from a pool of about 8.1M. It is free for the glasses user. — [Be My Eyes](https://bemyeyes.com/blog/be-my-eyes-on-ray-ban-meta)
- A May 2026 update added "Hey Meta, Be My Eyes with [name]" for trusted friends and family, and a Service Directory with company reps (Tesco, Sony, Amtrak, Hilton). Voice controls for mute, unmute, video and hang-up on calls are "coming soon". The action button can be mapped to a single function, such as a volunteer call or "describe what's around me". — [Meta Newsroom, May 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/meta-ai-wearables-changing-the-game-for-disabled-people/)
- Be My Eyes community stories:
  - Syed changed BIOS settings on a new laptop with a volunteer.
  - Scott plays video games with a volunteer guiding him: "I just call a volunteer, and they guide me through the game."
  - Nathan: "no more trying to line a phone up with something."
  - This is a promotional source, so drawbacks are likely underreported. — [Be My Eyes stories](https://www.bemyeyes.com/?p=21193543)
- Chancey Fleet (blind tech educator) said the integration gave her the freedom to navigate a crowded airport hands-free. — [Be My Eyes](https://bemyeyes.com/blog/be-my-eyes-on-ray-ban-meta)

**Meta "Detailed responses" setting**
- Meta AI on glasses is tuned for brief answers by default. "Detailed responses" is an opt-in toggle (Meta AI app → Device settings → Accessibility) that gives richer scene and object descriptions. It launched in May 2025 in the US and Canada. — [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/ray-ban-meta-glasses-meta-ai-detailed-responses-blind-low-vision/); [Meta, May 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/05/advancing-accessibility-meta/)
- The feature was designed by a blind Meta engineer (unnamed) and describes where objects, people and signage are. — [Irish Times, May 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/technology/consumer-tech/2026/05/14/i-was-gobsmacked-smart-toy-brings-new-independence-for-the-visually-impaired/)
- Vision Australia's example prompt is "Hey Meta, look and give me a detailed description", useful for things like menus with prices. — [Vision Australia](https://employmentservices.visionaustralia.org/technology-products/resources/using-technology/ray-ban-meta-glasses/how-to-use-the-look-and-ask-ai-feature-to-identify-objects)

**Real tasks and user quotes**
- Victor Andrews (totally blind, about one year of use) uses them to read:
  - brochures, street signs, indoor wall signs, licence plates and cooking instructions on packages
  - where a package came from, at his job
- He also uses "Hey Meta, what do you see?", reaches Aira via WhatsApp, and takes photos and video. He says they help "when I have nobody around to help me read or see things." — [ACB Next Generation](https://acbnextgeneration.org/?p=4269)
- Brian Manning (Vision Ireland IT trainer) uses them to:
  - tell fish fingers from other foods and similar tins apart
  - read menus for dishes and prices
  - pull the key figure from post (a bank deposit total) without reading every line
- Manning on the experience:
  - "I was gobsmacked by them."
  - On prompting: "They're like a teenager; the response you get depends on the language you use to interact."
  - Limitation: "sometimes you'll ask Meta to read the text and instead it'll analyse it."
  - The glasses complement a guide dog or cane rather than replace it. — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/technology/consumer-tech/2026/05/14/i-was-gobsmacked-smart-toy-brings-new-independence-for-the-visually-impaired/)
- Donald Overton (blinded US Army veteran) uses them to navigate airports and read menus: "I can now go out to dinner with my wife and have a normal date night." — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/meta-ai-wearables-changing-the-game-for-disabled-people/)
- Macular Society tester: the glasses help with reading bus numbers and timetables. — [Macular Society, June 2025](https://www.macularsociety.org/about/media/news/2025/june/how-could-meta-ai-s-glasses-help-you/)
- Thomas Panek (blind CEO of Lighthouse Guild) completed the NYC Half Marathon using Oakley Meta Vanguard. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/meta-ai-wearables-changing-the-game-for-disabled-people/)

**Other products**
- Envision Ally Solos (launched Aug 2025, built with Solos):
  - conversational Ally assistant that keeps context across turns
  - reads books, signs, menus and documents; describes scenes and people; sets reminders; checks calendars
  - open-ear audio, no screen, about 10 h battery, swappable USB-C arms
  - Envision's design aim: "a helpful companion that is always listening but not overwhelming." — [THIIS](https://thiis.co.uk/ai-powered-smart-glasses-launched-to-help-people-with-sight-loss-access-the-world-around-them/?rel=author)
- Ally Solos retail price is $599, essentially Solos AirGo V hardware with modified firmware. AppleVis users are mixed: one was "extremely underwhelmed", another ranked them behind the Meta glasses. — [AppleVis forum](https://applevis.com/comment/200558); [HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/ally-solos-smart-glasses-ai-blind-low-vision-users)
- Third-party apps on Meta glasses via the Wearables Device Access Toolkit:
  - OOrion: find objects (saved items such as keys and wallet), read text, detect obstacles, live audio guidance
  - Aira: professional visual interpreters — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/meta-ai-wearables-changing-the-game-for-disabled-people/)
- OrCam Read 3 (2023) launched at $2,490 (regular price $2,790). — [Glance](https://glance.eyesoneyecare.com/stories/2023-11-15/orcam-introduces-ai-powered-device-for-low-vision-and-visual-impairment/)
- Celeste ("Seleste") glasses were discussed in 2024 forums with a low upfront price of about $100–150. — [AppleVis](https://www.applevis.com/comment/164056)

**Errors and limits**
- A blind editor reported that Meta's glasses failed to correctly read the number on the door of his own home. — [Tildes discussion of news piece](https://tildes.net/~tech/1m2x/metas_ai_powered_ray_bans_are_life_enhancing_for_the_blind)

### Inferences
- What users value most is **hands-free, no phone-aiming**. Many of the tasks were possible before with phone apps, so the win is the form factor and the voice trigger.
- The best-loved feature is a **hybrid AI → human escalation ladder**: AI first, then a volunteer, a friend or family member, or a company rep.
- **Verbatim vs. summarise** is a real failure mode worth designing an explicit mode switch for.

### Gaps
- Seeing AI has no confirmed native glasses integration as of the sources found.
- Found no independent accuracy benchmark specific to Meta glasses for blind tasks.
- The "Aira Celeste" relationship is unclear; current Celeste and OrCam MyEye 3 pricing is unverified.
- Found no formal battery numbers under continuous Be My Eyes video calls.

## Deaf / hard of hearing: live captioning glasses

### Takeaway
A crowded market of captioning glasses priced $499–$799 (XanderGlasses at $5,000):
- XRAI AR2
- AirCaps (formerly TranscribeGlass)
- Captify
- LEION Hey2
- Meta Ray-Ban Display
- Even Realities G1 via apps

Captioning works best one-on-one in quiet settings. Speaker identification is usually paywalled or experimental, latency claims (under 300–500 ms) come from vendors, and group conversations and noise remain the hard problems.

### Cited Findings
- Product comparison from [HearingTracker](https://www.hearingtracker.com/resources/hear-with-your-eyes-five-ar-live-captioning-glasses):

  | Product | Price | Key points |
  |---|---|---|
  | XRAI AR2 | $699 | 3 mics, offline mode, 20 languages free; speaker ID only on the Ultimate plan ($30/mo) |
  | XanderGlasses | $5,000 | Standalone and offline; 85–95% accuracy offline, 97% online; 2.5–3 h battery; available through the VA for US veterans |
  | LEION Hey2 | $549 | Claims under 0.5 s latency and up to 98% accuracy; no camera, for privacy and battery reasons |
  | AirCaps | $499–699 | Experimental speaker ID via voice registration; iOS only |
  | Captify Pro | $699 | Environmental sound labels (applause, alarms); speaker differentiation is Premium-only; about 4 h binocular battery |
  | Meta Ray-Ban Display | $799 | English-only live and call captions; "not captioning-first assistive devices" |

- A Deaf XRAI user (about 3 weeks of use) said speaker ID works well but Basic mode "works very well", best one-on-one or with one speaker at a time. Performance depends on noise, speaker distance and accents. — [HearingTracker forum via search](https://forum.hearingtracker.com/t/-/110090/4)
- AirCaps/TranscribeGlass claims sub-300 ms latency (vendor claim). One outlet said text sometimes appears too quickly to read comfortably. — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/new-glasses-can-transcribe-speech-in-real-time/); [Yale Alumni Magazine](https://yalealumnimagazine.org/articles/6208)
- Meta Ray-Ban Display captions were extended in May 2026 to phone, WhatsApp, Messenger and Instagram voice calls, shown in the lens. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/meta-ai-wearables-changing-the-game-for-disabled-people/)
- Research on how captions should look and behave:
  - Kang et al. (CHI 2024 EA) list the persistent problems in glasses captioning: ASR delays, dropouts, heavy accents and mistranscriptions. They test giving *hearing partners* feedback when the Deaf or hard-of-hearing partner is missing content. — [Kang et al. CHI'24](https://jennakangg.github.io/assets/pdfs/ChiCaptioning.pdf)
  - Speechbubbles (CHI 2018 EA, 12 Deaf/hard-of-hearing participants): participants significantly preferred speech-bubble captions attached to speakers over traditional captions, including indicators for off-screen speakers. — [Peng et al.](https://pure.lib.cgu.edu.tw/en/publications/speechbubbles-enhancing-captioning-experiences-for-deaf-and-hard-/)
  - GlassEar (CHI 2015, 24 Deaf/hard-of-hearing participants): hearing aids don't restore sound localisation, so visualising sound direction on a head-mounted display was valued. — [Jain et al.](https://soundability.eecs.umich.edu/img/portfolio/Jain_GlassEar_CHI2015.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Who-is-speaking and where-is-the-sound** are the unmet needs (speaker attribution, direction arrows, non-speech sound labels).
- Design for the **conversation partner** too, for example a cue that tells them to slow down.

### Gaps
- No independent latency or word-error-rate benchmarks across products were found.
- Even Realities subtitle and Google captioning-glasses specifics were not verified.

## Cognitive / neurodivergent / memory

### Takeaway
The evidence is early but promising. The strongest result is CrossSense (University of Sussex; won the £1m Longitude Prize on Dementia, March 2026), with object-naming prompts on glasses. Autism and ADHD evidence is small and uncontrolled.

### Cited Findings
- CrossSense pairs camera glasses with the AI assistant "Wispy". It gives real-time verbal and floating-text prompts, guides people step by step through daily tasks, and uses conversation for reminiscence. — [Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-03-19-ai-smart-glasses-win-dementia-longitude-prize/)
- CrossSense study: 23 pairs of people with dementia and their carers. Household-item naming rose from 46% without the glasses to 82% with them, and stayed at 78% an hour after removal. — [Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-03-19-ai-smart-glasses-win-dementia-longitude-prize/)
- CrossSense rollout and cost:
  - Battery is about 1 h, so a power bank is needed (flagged as an adoption barrier).
  - Glasses cost up to £1,000, plus about £50/month.
  - Plan: direct-to-consumer first, NHS later; a 4-week home pilot later in 2026; phone version end of 2026; glasses early 2027.
  - Dr Foyzul Rahman (Loughborough) calls for randomised trials and raises ethics concerns about data from vulnerable users. — [Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-03-19-ai-smart-glasses-win-dementia-longitude-prize/)
- The CrossSense results are not yet peer-reviewed. — [Optimist Daily](https://www.optimistdaily.com/2026/04/how-ai-powered-smart-glasses-could-transform-dementia-care-by-2027/)
- CareYaya announced AI smart glasses for dementia patients in Oct 2024. — [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2024/10/29/careyaya-unveils-ai-powered-smart-glasses-to-assist-dementia-patients)
- Empowered Brain (Brain Power) pilot for autistic people with ADHD symptoms: hyperactivity scores fell 54.9% in the high-symptom group and 20% in the low-symptom group 24 h after use. Small, with no control group. — [Optometry Times](https://optometrytimes.com/view/smart-glasses-technology-may-help-treat-patients-autism-adhd)

### Inferences
- Effective cognitive support looks like **contextual, just-in-time, step-by-step prompts with a light touch**, plus a carer in the loop.
- Strong ethics design is needed: consent from people with fluctuating capacity, and handling of data about vulnerable users.

### Gaps
- No rigorous sources were found on ADHD task-reminder glasses or on autism social-cue apps from 2023–2026.

## Motor impairments: hands-free, voice, EMG

### Takeaway
Voice ("Hey Meta"), single-button shortcuts and the Meta Neural Band (EMG) are the main channels. EMG is in research with Carnegie Mellon for people with spinal cord injury. Independent user testing with motor-impaired people is lacking.

### Cited Findings
- Noah Currier (Marine veteran with quadriplegia) takes photos and video by voice: "Being able to take them hands-free was incredible." — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/meta-ai-wearables-changing-the-game-for-disabled-people/)
- Meta has a three-year partnership with Carnegie Mellon on Neural Band EMG for spinal cord injury. Meta says the band detects forearm muscle signals "even in people who have been paralyzed for many years". Participant Cass raced a player using a standard controller by using two Neural Bands. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/meta-ai-wearables-changing-the-game-for-disabled-people/)
- A reviewer hopes the band "will learn to adapt to individual movement patterns, however small and faint". — [Optimizer newsletter mirror](https://thevergenews.pages.dev/column/781772/optimizer-newsletter-meta-ray-ban-display-smart-glasses-accessibility)
- Meta Ray-Ban Display added neural handwriting (typing by finger movements). — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/computing/metas-ray-ban-display-now-types-messages-from-your-finger-movements)

### Inferences
- Offer **redundant input paths**: voice, button, EMG and head gestures, so that no single modality is required. Voice fails for users with dysarthria, and EMG gestures fail for users who have none.

### Gaps
- No head-gesture data or independent EMG accessibility studies were found.

## Aging / elderly

### Takeaway
There is little elderly-specific evidence. Most relevant is age-related sight loss (Macular Society, RNIB) and dementia (CrossSense). Research samples skew young.

### Cited Findings
- The Macular Society (age-related macular disease population) promoted Meta glasses for reading bus numbers and timetables. — [Macular Society](https://www.macularsociety.org/about/media/news/2025/june/how-could-meta-ai-s-glasses-help-you/)
- The ASSETS'24 "Misfitting" study had only one participant over 65, an explicit limitation. — [Alharbi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546v1)
- Setup friction is a barrier: a Meta account and app are required, users are advised to install the app before buying, and no charging plug is included. — [ACB Next Generation](https://acbnextgeneration.org/?p=4269)

### Gaps
- Found no studies on older adults' acceptance of AI glasses, fall detection, or medication reminders.

## Design principles (verbosity, multimodal, privacy, error transparency)

### Takeaway
The evidence points to the following:
- adjustable verbosity (brief by default, detail on demand)
- an explicit read-verbatim vs. summarise mode
- spoken uncertainty and abstention
- camera-framing guidance
- contestability
- escalation to a human
- redundant input and output channels
- bystander-privacy signalling beyond an LED

### Cited Findings
- **Verbosity:** Meta tunes for brevity and adds an opt-in "detailed responses" toggle. — [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/ray-ban-meta-glasses-meta-ai-detailed-responses-blind-low-vision/)
- **Calibrated uncertainty:** The ACL 2025 study (University of Copenhagen and Heriot-Watt; 13 models; tasks co-designed with blind people) found that the hardest questions got the most confident answers. Braille and assistive-device recognition were weak, and non-English performance dropped.
  - Søgaard: "If systems sound certain while they are in fact guessing, they blur the line between knowledge and guesswork" and "we risk making people less independent by giving them something they cannot trust."
  - Recommendations: reward abstention, and prompt users to retake the photo or ask more precisely. — [ScienceNews.dk](https://www.sciencenews.dk/en/when-ai-sounds-certain-blind-users-have-to-trust-it-even-when-it-is-guessing)
- **How blind users verify AI output:** ASSETS'24 "Misfitting With AI" (26 blind or low-vision users) found they verify by:
  - experimenting in familiar settings
  - non-visual sensing (shaking cans, touch)
  - asking sighted people for high-stakes checks
  - cross-referencing apps
- Example errors from the same study:
  - "10/01" read as the wrong date format
  - "dot com" read as "dot corn"
  - a white cane called a "hiking pole", repeated even after correction
  - tables and recipes read out of order
- Design implications from the study:
  - directional camera guidance ("move left by...")
  - real-time warnings about blur or obstruction
  - contestable corrections that persist
  - confidence indicators (users were divided, and want to know how confidence is calculated)
  - saving outputs to cross-check
- Participant quotes: "Just play with it. Don't rely on them" (P20); AI is "more of a guidance thing than a full support thing" (P26). — [Alharbi et al., ASSETS'24](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06546v1)
- 20 blind GenAI users showed "everyday uncertainty", a skeptical mindset toward AI. — [MDSOAR](https://mdsoar.org/items/ca315be6-364c-4c5d-9356-c515c0f9f06d)
- **Bystander privacy:** The recording LED can be covered after recording starts, and critics say the burden of noticing falls on bystanders. Meta has also faced EU pushback over AI training data. — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/technology/consumer-tech/2026/05/14/i-was-gobsmacked-smart-toy-brings-new-independence-for-the-visually-impaired/)
- **Camera-free design as a privacy choice:** LEION Hey2 omits the camera for privacy and battery reasons. HearingTracker flags privacy concerns for Meta Display. — [HearingTracker](https://www.hearingtracker.com/resources/hear-with-your-eyes-five-ar-live-captioning-glasses)
- **Companion tone:** Envision's aim is "always listening but not overwhelming", with no screen. — [THIIS](https://thiis.co.uk/ai-powered-smart-glasses-launched-to-help-people-with-sight-loss-access-the-world-around-them/?rel=author)

### Inferences (hackathon-relevant)
- **Safety-critical gating:** for medication, dates, money and door numbers, read verbatim, state confidence, and offer a "verify with a human" one-tap path.
- **Same content, multiple outputs:** audio for blind users, text in the lens for Deaf users, short step cards for cognitive support. Let the user pick the output and the detail level.
- **Inclusive by default:** features built for disability (captions, hands-free capture) appeal broadly; reviewers note captions have "enormous potential" beyond Deaf and hard-of-hearing users.

### Gaps
- Found no published consent framework specific to assistive glasses (for example, face recognition of bystanders for blind users).

## Pilots and partnerships (path to adoption)

### Takeaway
Adoption runs through blindness charities and nonprofits, veterans' programmes and prize-funded research, more than through formal NHS or school pilots. Formal clinical trials are just starting.

### Cited Findings
- Meta's partners:
  - Be My Eyes (since 2024)
  - Aira and OOrion via the Device Access Toolkit
  - Lighthouse Guild and Achilles International (Meta Lab NYC event, 21 May 2026; a Bike Club for blind riders)
  - Carnegie Mellon (EMG research) — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/05/meta-ai-wearables-changing-the-game-for-disabled-people/)
- RNIB's Low Vision Centre offers try-before-you-buy for Ray-Ban Meta glasses, including prescription lenses, by appointment. The booking contact is an NHS address. — [RNIB Shop](https://shop.rnib.org.uk/blogs/news/try-ray-ban-meta-glasses-with-prescription-lenses-at-rnib)
- The Blinded Veterans Association published a Meta glasses training guide for blind and low-vision users (Nov 2025). — [BVA PDF](https://bva.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Meta-Glasses-Training-Guide-for-Blind-and-Low-Vision-Users.pdf)
- XanderGlasses are available through the VA, so eligible US veterans may get them free. — [HearingTracker](https://www.hearingtracker.com/resources/hear-with-your-eyes-five-ar-live-captioning-glasses)
- Clinical trial NCT07317180, "Meta Glasses for Low Vision" (London Health Sciences Centre, Canada), was not yet enrolling. — [Veeva CTV](https://ctv.veeva.com/study/meta-glasses-for-low-vision)
- Envision partnered with Aira to bring live agents to Envision Glasses. — [Envision blog](https://letsenvision.com/blog/envision-and-aira-announce-strategic-partnership-to-enhance-envision-glasses-for-people-who-are-blind-or-have-low-vision)
- CrossSense plans NHS availability after direct-to-consumer sales. — [Resultsense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-03-19-ai-smart-glasses-win-dementia-longitude-prize/)

### Gaps
- No confirmed NHS or school pilot of AI glasses was found.
- Envision's healthcare-organisation partnerships were not verified.
